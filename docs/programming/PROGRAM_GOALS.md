## Who it's for

Court6 is a CrossFit-style group-class program that also quietly builds HYROX-style fitness.

- **Members:** mostly general population; many are masters (40+), and few race.
- **Schedule:** classes are 60 minutes, Monday to Friday. No weekend classes and no partner work.
- **Language:** member text never names HYROX.

## Design principles (all cycles)

- Lift or skill first, conditioning second.
- About two hard days a week (Monday and Friday). Wednesday is "sustain", not empty-the-tank. Thursday is low intensity but engaging.
- **Strength:**
  - 2–3 main lifts a week, each trained once a week in a fixed slot, 3–5 hard sets.
  - Percentages of estimated max with an RPE 8 cap.
  - e1RM = 3RM × 1.06 (press: 5RM × 1.13).
- **Running:**
  - Class running rises about 10% a week and never more than 20–30% in any 2-week window (Nielsen 2014; Damsted 2019; the 10% figure is a conservative convention).
  - After a deload, go back to the pre-deload volume, not higher.
- **Skill:** one per cycle, built strength → strict → kip, with a baseline and a retest.
- Program for the majority and scale by load, reps, distance and time cap, not by writing a different workout.
- Publish the plan so members see each day's purpose and the retest wins.

## Year shape: six 9-week cycles

See [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). Each cycle runs test → Base → Build → Primer → Retest → Flex.

The variety comes between cycles. Each cycle trains its own lifts and one skill:

| Cycle | Tested lifts | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Back squat, deadlift, strict press | Toes-to-bar |
| 2 | Maintenance | Double-unders |
| 3 | Front squat, deadlift, bench press | Strict and chest-to-bar pull-ups |
| 4 | Engine cycle | Handstands and handstand push-ups |
| 5 | Clean & jerk | Bar muscle-up |
| 6 | Cycle 1 battery retest | Ring muscle-up progression |

The Nov 22 research found this is the standard shape for class programs: 2–4 lifts held steady for one cycle, with the lift set changing between cycles. Keep each tested lift weekly within its cycle, because a 3RM depends heavily on practising that exact lift. Put the variety in secondary slots, accessories and conditioning.

## Planning notes for Cycle 2 and later

These come from the Nov 22 review of the roadmap against the research and the rules.

**Cycle 2 (starts Mon Dec 21):**
- The roadmap calls it maintenance without naming lifts. About one weekly exposure per lift at a decent intensity holds strength.
- With no strength retest at the end, it's the safe cycle to rotate variants: front squat, push press, sumo or trap-bar deadlift.
- The holidays fall in Weeks 1–2 (Dec 25 and Jan 1 are Fridays). Avoid putting tests in that window.
- If the lifts stay the same, the Cycle 1 Week 8 numbers can be the baseline.
- The engine test is a 3 km run or 3,000 m row (decided Nov 22: fits the 60-min class; a 5 km may come later in the year). See CYCLE2_DESIGN.md.

**Cycles 3–6:**
- Keep back squat and strict press as weekly secondary lifts through Cycles 3–5. Otherwise the Cycle 6 "how far you've come" retest will under-read real progress.
- **Race timing:** HYROX Ottawa (Apr 21–25, 2027) falls in the last week of Cycle 3, a strength cycle. The original plan had a 9-week race block from about Feb 15. Decide whether any members are racing before writing Cycle 3.
- **Friday:** it carries both power clean and strict press. Alternating them week to week (A/B) frees class time.
- **Muscle-ups (Cycles 5–6):** a big ask for masters. Build toward them from Cycle 3's strict pulling, and plan the lower levels as progression steps.
- **Named benchmarks:** Fran, Grace, Diane and Isabel must match the official workout at Full, or the preview check blocks them. Diane's handstand push-ups line up with Cycle 4's skill.

## Research background

Useful patterns from public programs and the research (details in the report and `research_notes/`):

- Most class programs run 6–12-week cycles, test → build → back-off/primer → retest. Court6's 9 weeks sits in the common range.
- Test both strength and engine, and write the percentages and paces from those tests. Add a short named benchmark every 2–4 weeks.
- A fixed weekly rhythm works better than daily randomness. Pin lift families to days within a cycle.
- **HYROX-style work, done quietly:**
  - One threshold run, one easier aerobic day, a stations or compromised-running day, and a simulation near the end.
  - No sled or SkiErg at Court6: use burpees, carries, lunges and rower/bike instead.
- Conditioning doesn't hurt maximal strength, but it does reduce power gains when both are in the same session. That's one more reason power cleans come first in class.
- **Retention:** visible retest wins, a stable class flow and community matter more than novelty. Variety helps adherence when it comes from formats and secondary work.
