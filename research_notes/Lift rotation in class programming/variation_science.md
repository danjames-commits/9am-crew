# Exercise Variation vs. Repeating the Same Lifts Across a Training Block

Context: general-population and masters (40+) adults, about 5 days/week, 60-minute group classes that also include conditioning. Question: should a block repeat 3 main lifts weekly (fixed lift per day) or rotate a larger pool (A/B weeks, primary + variation lifts)?

Evidence grading used below: **[Strong]** = meta-analysis or multiple consistent RCTs; **[Moderate]** = single RCTs or small reviews; **[Expert opinion]** = coaching literature, program authors, commentary; **[Unverified]** = could only see abstract or secondary summary.

## 1. What do systematic reviews and RCTs say about exercise variation and strength/hypertrophy?

### Takeaway
Across the small body of trials (about 8 studies in the main review, mostly young men, 8 to 12 weeks), moderate, planned variation produces strength and hypertrophy outcomes that are about the same as a fixed exercise menu, sometimes slightly better for regional hypertrophy. Excessive or random variation may undercut gains, especially in skill-heavy lifts. Variation is not needed for results, and it does not clearly hurt them when the tested lift keeps getting trained.

### Cited Findings
- **Kassiano et al. 2022 (JSCR 36(6):1753-1762), systematic review [Moderate, small evidence base]:** searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science; 8 studies met inclusion; pooled N = 241, all young men; PEDro quality "good" to "excellent." Conclusion: some systematic variation seems to improve regional hypertrophy and maximize dynamic strength, but excessive, random variation may compromise gains. The authors recommend that variation follow anatomical and biomechanical logic. Redundant exercises or changing exercises too often may hinder adaptation. — [PubMed 35438660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/) (abstract via search snippet; full text not retrieved)
- Commentary on Kassiano 2022: it says neither "variation is good" nor "variation is bad." Variation should be managed carefully for skill-dependent lifts like the squat, and "varying other exercises will likely not negatively affect strength gains." Two of the included studies found no strength difference between varied and fixed groups. — [Biolayne Reps, Issue 7](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/) [Expert opinion]
- **Fonseca et al. 2014 (JSCR 28(11):3085-3092) [Moderate]:** 49 physically active participants in 4 groups: constant intensity/constant exercise (CICE), constant intensity/varied exercise (CIVE), varied intensity/constant exercise (VICE), varied intensity/varied exercise (VIVE). CIVE and VIVE showed hypertrophy in all quadriceps heads (p <= 0.05). CICE did not show it in vastus medialis and rectus femoris, and VICE did not show it in rectus femoris. Authors concluded that varying exercises was more effective for strength gains in active individuals. Once intensity passes a threshold, hypertrophy is similar regardless of intensity or exercise variation. Varied exercises were squat, leg press, deadlift, lunge, rotated across the program. — [BVS record mdl-24832974](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-24832974); [Gentil PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Changes%20in%20exercises%20are%20more%20effective%20than%20in%20loading%20schemes%20to%20improve%20muscle%20strength.pdf) [Unverified: exact effect sizes not retrieved; exercise list from my prior knowledge]
- Fonseca's earlier dissertation (untrained men, 12 weeks): adding exercise variation, alone or with intensity variation, produced no extra gains in strength or CSA. — [USP thesis](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/39/39132/tde-25052011-153336/en.php) [Moderate; contrasts with the 2014 trained/active result]
- **Baz-Valle et al. 2019 (PLOS ONE) [Moderate]:** 21 resistance-trained men, 8 weeks, fixed exercises (CON) vs exercises randomly varied each session by an app (RAN). Both groups improved similarly in muscle thickness and maximal strength. Varying exercises "had a positive effect on enhancing motivation to train." — [PubMed 31881066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31881066/); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0226989). The RAN group's motivation scores rose pre to post while CON's slightly declined. — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/study-exercise-variation). Examine cautions this is a single 8-week study. — [Examine](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/how-often-should-i-switch-up-my-exercise-routine) [Unverified: exact 1RM percentages and effect sizes not retrieved; PMC full text blocked by bot check]
- **Kassiano et al. 2024 (young women) [Moderate, largest RCT]:** 90 enrolled, 70 completed (CON n = 38, VAR n = 32), untrained at least 6 months, lower body 3x/week for 10 weeks, 2 sets of 10-15. CON did leg press + stiff-leg deadlift every session. VAR did that on Monday, hack squat + lying leg curl on Wednesday, and Smith squat + seated leg curl on Friday. Muscle thickness +7.8-17.7% (CON) vs +7.5-19.3% (VAR). 1RM +24.4-32.1% (CON) vs +29.0-30.1% (VAR). Tested on leg press (trained in both groups) and leg extension (untrained). Conclusion: "virtually similar" strength and hypertrophy. — [ECU Research Online](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912); [StrengthLog summary](https://www.strengthlog.com/varied-vs-constant-exercises/)
- Note on the test design in Kassiano 2024: the VAR group still did the tested lift (leg press) once a week versus three times for CON, and got similar 1RM leg-press gains. In novices, one weekly exposure to the tested lift plus similar movements was enough. — inference from [StrengthLog summary](https://www.strengthlog.com/varied-vs-constant-exercises/)
- StrengthLog's take: choosing good exercises matters more than variation. For squat/bench/deadlift strength, both a specialized approach centered on the lift and a varied approach with similar accessories are viable. — [StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/varied-vs-constant-exercises/) [Expert opinion]
- **Periodization meta-analysis, Moesgaard et al. 2022 (Sports Med) [Strong]:** periodized > non-periodized for 1RM (ES 0.31, 95% CI 0.04-0.57). Undulating > linear for 1RM (ES 0.31, 95% CI 0.02-0.61), but only in trained individuals. No hypertrophy difference linear vs undulating (ES 0.05). Block periodization was not pooled because there were too few studies. Untrained participants benefited regardless of variation approach. — [PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/); [U. Copenhagen press release](https://news.ku.dk/all_news/2022/04/varied-strength-training-programmes-increase-strength-but-not-muscle-mass). This is variation of *loading* (sets/reps/intensity), not of *exercise selection*, which is a separate lever.
- Harries et al. 2015 meta-analysis: no difference between linear and undulating periodization for upper- or lower-body strength. — cited via [Examine / Moesgaard search summary](https://examine.com/faq/are-periodized-training-programs-superior-for-strength-gains) [Unverified]

### Inferences
- The trials generally test variation *around* the tested lift: the tested lift still appears regularly, and rotated lifts train the same muscles with similar patterns. None of them show that dropping the tested lift for weeks preserves its 1RM gains.
- For novices and general-population lifters, the choice between "3 fixed lifts" and "rotated pool" is roughly neutral for strength and size. Second-order factors (motivation, joint tolerance, test validity, coaching logistics) can reasonably decide it.
- Hypertrophy may slightly favor a small pool of complementary exercises (regional growth, e.g. adding knee-dominant + hip-dominant + isolation). Strength in a specific barbell lift favors keeping that lift in the rotation often.

### Gaps
- "Costa et al. 2021" could not be identified as a separate exercise-variation trial. Costa appears as a co-author on Kassiano 2022/2024 and on a 2021 Frontiers in Physiology paper surfaced in search ([Frontiers 2021.789403](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.789403/epub)) that I did not open. Verify before citing.
- The full list of 8 studies in Kassiano 2022 and their individual effect sizes were not retrieved. Rauch et al. 2020 (likely one of them) was not verified.
- No trials found in masters (40+) populations comparing fixed vs varied exercise selection. All evidence is from young adults.
- No trial longer than about 12 weeks, so long-term effects (6+ months) are unknown.

## 2. Specificity, skill, and how often a lift must be trained

### Takeaway
Strength, especially 1RM in a barbell lift, is highly task-specific. A large share of early 1RM gains comes from practicing that exact movement. When volume is equated, weekly frequency does not affect strength much, so a lift trained hard once a week can keep progressing if weekly volume per muscle is adequate. Doing the tested lift more often is a cheap way to add practice, though.

### Cited Findings
- **Mattocks et al. 2017 (Med Sci Sports Exerc) [Moderate]:** a group that only practiced the 1RM test (up to 5 single attempts per visit) gained upper- and lower-body 1RM similarly to a high-volume training group, despite far less volume. Volume and muscle growth did not explain the extra strength. — [PubMed 28463902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463902/)
- **Dankel et al. 2017 [Moderate]:** over 21 days, 1RM rose similarly in a trained arm and a test-only arm, though only the trained arm grew. Authors attribute strength gains to specificity and mechanisms other than hypertrophy. — [PubMed 27875635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27875635/)
- A 151-participant study found only the 1RM-test group increased untrained-arm 1RM. The authors argued strength is largely driven by task specificity. — [Spitz et al., ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Robert-Spitz/publication/336068902_Assessing_differential_responders_and_mean_changes_in_muscle_size_strength_and_the_cross-over_effect_to_two_distinct_resistance_training_protocols/links/5dbae8e1a6fdcc2128f1a3dc/Assessing-differential-responders-and-mean-changes-in-muscle-size-strength-and-the-cross-over-effect-to-two-distinct-resistance-training-protocols.pdf)
- **Grgic et al. 2018 (Sports Med), frequency and strength meta-analysis [Strong]:** strength gains rose with weekly frequency in the unadjusted analysis (pooled ES about 0.74, 0.82, 0.93, 1.08 for 1, 2, 3, 4+ times/week), but there was no significant frequency effect in volume-equated studies (p = 0.421). Effects "seem to be primarily driven by training volume." — [PMC6081873 / search summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6081873) [ES values from search snippet; subgroup results (multi-joint vs single-joint, upper vs lower, older adults) not verified. My recollection is that higher frequency showed an advantage for multi-joint and upper-body exercises in the non-equated analysis; treat this as unverified]
- **Ralston et al. 2018 meta-analysis [Strong]:** volume-equated low vs high frequency produced similar strength gains (mean ES difference 0.03). — [Research portal UWS](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/weekly-training-frequency-effects-on-strength-gain-a-meta-analysi/)
- **Saric et al. 2018 [Moderate]:** 3 vs 6 sessions/week, volume-equated, trained men, 6 weeks: similar strength gains. — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Jozo-Grgic/publication/327435932_Resistance_Training_Frequencies_of_3_and_6_Times_Per_Week_Produce_Similar_Muscular_Adaptations_in_Resistance-Trained_Men/links/5bd26d6892851c6b278f311d/Resistance-Training-Frequencies-of-3-and-6-Times-Per-Week-Produce-Similar-Muscular-Adaptations-in-Resistance-Trained-Men.pdf)
- Hypertrophy frequency (Schoenfeld et al. 2016 meta-analysis): reviewed as a separate analysis. — [PubMed 27102172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) [details not retrieved]
- NSCA SCJ 2022 article on "transfer between lifts" exists and defines specificity as adaptations matching the stress applied. — [NSCA SCJ PDF](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/3c0357cb66b54c6b9a598b0ca578600a/scj-transfer-between-lifts-2022.pdf) [PDF text not extractable; contents unverified]

### Inferences
- For a block that measures 3RM squat/deadlift/press, each tested lift should appear at least once a week (ideally with some exposure in the tested rep range) to capture the skill/practice part of the strength gain. Fixed lift-per-day gives exactly one weekly exposure per lift. That is enough per the volume-equated frequency data, if weekly sets are adequate.
- Rotating a variation (e.g. front squat or box squat in place of back squat) still builds the muscle, but it pays less of the "practice the test" dividend. A rotated design would likely show smaller 3RM gains on the tested lift even if underlying strength is similar.
- In novices, the skill component is the largest share of early gains, so specificity matters *more* for them on the tested lift, not less.

### Gaps
- No direct data on minimum effective dose of the *specific* lift (vs similar lifts) needed to maintain 1RM in novices over a 4-8 week block.
- Detraining/maintenance literature (e.g., maintaining strength with one session/week or 1/3 volume) was not retrieved in this pass.

## 3. Established periodization models and how many lifts they rotate

### Takeaway
Mainstream coach-designed programs mostly keep a small number of main lifts fixed (3-4 barbell lifts trained 1-3x/week) and put variety into loading and accessories. Conjugate/Westside is the main exception: it rotates max-effort variations every 1-3 weeks, but it was built for advanced (often equipped) powerlifters and still retests on a cycle. Alternation patterns (A/B days) mostly exist to manage recovery between similar lifts (bench vs press), not to add novelty.

### Cited Findings
- **Starting Strength (novice) [Expert opinion]:** Workouts A and B alternate across 3 non-consecutive days (A/B/A then B/A/B). A = squat, press, deadlift. B = squat, bench, power clean. 3x5 squat and presses, 1x5 deadlift. Squat is trained every session. Press and bench alternate so each gets recovery. — [FitnessVolt](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/starting-strength/); [Barbell Logic](https://barbell-logic.com/starting-strength-novice-linear-progression/)
- **Texas Method (intermediate) [Expert opinion]:** 3 sessions/week of the same core lifts: volume day (e.g. 5x5 squat, 5x5 bench or press), a lighter recovery day, and an intensity day (one heavy set of 5 for a 5RM PR). The weekly variation is in load and volume, not exercise. — [Starting Strength: The Texas Method](https://mail.startingstrength.com/article/the_texas_method); [Liftosaur](https://liftosaur.com/programs/texasmethod). Sources say it is for lifters past novice linear progression (some suggest 12-24+ months training). — [FitnessVolt](https://fitnessvolt.com/strength-gains-texas-method/)
- **Wendler 5/3/1 [Expert opinion]:** 4 main lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press), one per day, 4 days/week (3-day variants exist). 4-week wave: 5s, 3s, 5/3/1+ (AMRAP top set), then deload. Boring But Big: 5x10 of the same lift at about 50-60% after main sets, plus 1 assistance movement (lats/abs). Main lifts stay fixed across cycles. Variety lives in templates and assistance. — [Boostcamp BBB](https://boostcamp.app/coaches/jim-wendler/5-3-1-boring-but-big); [Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/the531bbb); [Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/node/4458) [secondary sources; Wendler's books not consulted]
- **Westside/Conjugate [Expert opinion]:** 4-day template: max-effort (ME) lower, ME upper, dynamic-effort (DE) lower, DE upper. ME lower on Monday and DE lower on Friday. ME exercise is rotated to avoid accommodation: one article says changed weekly, another says the standard is 4 ME variations per month. DE work runs in 3-week pendulum waves (e.g., DE upper 9x3 for all three weeks). Westside "would rotate back to test the same exercise every nine weeks." A 3-day template with a combined DE day is used for sport athletes. — [Westside: How to rotate max effort](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/how-to-rotate-max-effort); [Basic template breakdown](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/the-basic-template-breakdown); [Starting conjugate: exercise variation](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/starting-conjugate-exercise-variation); [DE guide](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/the-wsbb-guide-to-dynamic-effort-training)
- **GZCL [Expert opinion]:** tiered structure. T1 = main competition lift (squat/bench/deadlift/OHP), usually 1 per session, 1-3 rep sets (GZCLP uses 5x3 progressing). T2 = 1-2 related lifts per session, about 20-30 total reps in sets of 5-8. T3 = higher-rep accessories, 1-3 (original) or 3-4 (P-Zero) per session. Volume ratio about 1:2:3 (T1:T2:T3), described as a guideline. The T1 lifts are fixed. T2 is where close variations (e.g., front squat, Romanian deadlift) live. — [GZCL Substack P-Zero](https://gzcl.substack.com/p/p-zero); [Swole at every height 2014](https://swoleateveryheight.blogspot.com/2014/07/the-gzcl-method-simplified_13.html); [Boostcamp GZCLP](https://boostcamp.app/gzcl-method-gzclp-program-app)
- **Periodization models (linear/DUP/block):** undulating beats linear only in trained people (ES 0.31). Block has too little data for meta-analysis. — [Moesgaard 2022, PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/)

### Inferences
- A "primary + variation" structure (fixed T1 lift per day, rotating T2/T3) matches GZCL, 5/3/1 assistance, and the evidence. Rotating the primary lift itself (conjugate-style) is a minority, advanced-athlete approach.
- An A/B-week alternation of primaries (e.g., back squat week A, front squat week B) cuts each tested lift's exposure to about every other week. That is less frequent than any of the mainstream novice/intermediate models above.
- Starting Strength's A/B pattern alternates *within* the week and still trains the squat every session. Its press/bench alternation gives each press about 1.5x/week.

### Gaps
- Olympic weightlifting cycle structures (e.g., how many snatch/clean variants per block) were not researched in this pass.
- Primary sources (Wendler books, Simmons' Westside Barbell Book of Methods, Rippetoe's Practical Programming) were not consulted directly; details come from program sites and secondary summaries.
- The Westside "1-3 week" rotation rule was not found stated verbatim; sources say weekly rotation, 4 per month, and 3-week DE waves.

## 4. Retest validity when lifts rotate mid-cycle

### Takeaway
If the block tests 3RM squat/deadlift/press at start and end, rotating those exact lifts out mid-cycle mixes two things in the result: real strength change and lost practice/specificity. Practice alone can produce 1RM gains as big as higher-volume training. So a design with fewer exposures to the test lift will tend to under-show progress, and the test may partly measure familiarity rather than strength.

### Cited Findings
- Practicing the 1RM test alone produced strength gains equal to a high-volume program. — [Mattocks 2017, PubMed 28463902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463902/)
- 1RM gains occurred in a test-only arm without hypertrophy, attributed to task specificity. — [Dankel 2017, PubMed 27875635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27875635/)
- Westside, despite heavy ME rotation, rotates back to test the same exercise about every 9 weeks to assess long-term progress. In practice, the test lift is periodically re-exposed. — [Westside: How to rotate max effort](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/how-to-rotate-max-effort)
- In Kassiano 2024, the varied group trained the tested lift once a week (vs 3x) and still matched leg-press 1RM gains in untrained women. A moderate reduction in test-lift exposure did not degrade measured progress over 10 weeks in novices. — [ECU Research Online](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912); [StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/varied-vs-constant-exercises/)
- Learning effects with repeated 1RM tests are a known issue in measurement. — [NeuRA: learning effect with 1RM tests](https://motorimpairment.neura.edu.au/?p=2950) [not opened]

### Inferences
- To keep the 3RM retest valid, keep each tested lift in the program at least weekly, ideally with some heavy (about 3-5 rep) exposure in the 1-2 weeks before the retest. Rotate variations in secondary slots.
- If primaries must rotate (A/B weeks), schedule the final 1-2 weeks so each tested lift appears in its tested form before the retest, which works like a re-familiarization or peaking phase.
- Retesting novice adults always includes a skill/learning component. A fixed-lift block will show larger "gains" partly because of that, so comparisons across blocks with different structures are not apples-to-apples.

### Gaps
- No studies were found directly comparing pre/post 3RM results in groups that removed the tested lift for part of a block vs kept it in.

## 5. Masters/general-population considerations: joints, repetitive strain, adherence, enjoyment

### Takeaway
Variety helps adherence and motivation in the best available (small) experiments. It also lets coaches swap joint-friendly variants, which fits NSCA guidance for older adults. There is no direct evidence that repeating the same 3 barbell lifts causes repetitive strain injuries in masters lifters. NSCA reports injuries in older adults are linked mainly to inexperience, heavy loads, poor technique, and inappropriate exercise selection, which argues for consistent technique practice as much as for variety.

### Cited Findings
- **Sylvester et al. 2016 (J Behav Med) [Moderate]:** 121 inactive university students randomized to high vs low variety-support exercise for 6 weeks. High variety had better adherence (F(1,116) = 5.55, p = .02). Mediated by perceived variety (beta = .16, p < .01). — [Univ. of Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/variety-support-and-exercise-adherence-behavior-experimental-and-/); [UBC news](https://yre.educ.ubc.ca/new-publication-from-drs-crocker-zumbo-and-beauchamp-variety-support-and-exercise-adherence-behavior-experimental-and-mediating-effects/)
- Related Sylvester work: perceived variety predicted exercise-related well-being, and in a cross-sectional study of 499 adults, it predicted exercise behavior through autonomous motivation. — [Sylvester et al. 2016, Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being (PDF)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Sylvester_et_al-2016-Applied_Psychology-_Health_and_Well-Being.pdf); [UBC dissertation](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/24/items/1.0224103)
- Baz-Valle 2019: random exercise variation increased motivation to train in trained men, with similar muscular outcomes. — [PubMed 31881066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31881066/); [UPV/EHU news](https://www.ehu.eus/en/web/campusa-magazine/-/doing-exercises-randomly-improves-motivation)
- **NSCA position statement, older adults (Fragala et al. 2019, JSCR 33(8)) [Strong, consensus]:**
  - **Frequency, sets, intensity:** 2-3 days/week. 2-3 sets per major muscle group, 2 min rest. Intensity progresses from low (<51% 1RM) to moderate (51-69%) to high (70-85%) for some clients. Training to failure has not been shown to add adaptations in older adults. 50-70% 1RM with excellent form may be enough for many.
  - **Exercise selection:** 8-10 exercises. Machines are an appropriate start for frail or beginner adults. Free weights offer greater benefit for high-functioning older adults.
  - **Power and progression:** Power training at 40-60% 1RM. Use an individualized, periodized progression.
  - **Joints and injury:** Take special care with the shoulder complex, knee, hip, and spine. For osteoarthritis, adjust exercise selection, intensity, volume, and range of motion. Most injuries occur in inexperienced individuals and relate to heavy loads, poor technique, and inappropriate exercise selection.
  - Sources: [PubMed 31343601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31343601/); [Kravitz summary, UNM](https://www.unm.edu/~lkravitz/Article%20folder/NSCApositionstand.html)
- BarBend's reading of Baz-Valle: moderate variation suits adherence goals, and less variation may be better when maximizing a specific strength outcome. — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/study-exercise-variation) [Expert opinion]

### Inferences
- In a group-class setting, motivation and attendance likely matter more for real-world results than the small strength differences between fixed and varied designs. Variety in the *session* (conditioning formats, accessories, variation slots) may give most of the perceived-variety benefit without rotating the tested lifts.
- The NSCA's joint-specific cautions support offering scalable variants of each main pattern: box squat or goblet squat for knee pain, trap-bar or block pulls for back issues, landmine press for shoulder issues. This is variation by *individual need*, not rotation for its own sake.
- Since technique and inexperience drive injuries in older adults, repeated practice of a few lifts may actually reduce injury risk for novices. Many novel variations each week could raise it. This is a reasoned inference, not tested.

### Gaps
- No studies found on repetitive-strain or overuse injury rates from repeating the same barbell lifts in masters athletes.
- Variety/adherence studies (Sylvester) used young inactive students and general exercise, not resistance-training classes or 40+ adults.
- No masters-specific RCT on exercise variation for strength found.

## 6. Interference / concurrent training: how many strength exposures fit alongside conditioning

### Takeaway
The best meta-analysis finds no meaningful interference of conditioning with maximal strength or whole-muscle hypertrophy. It does find a small-to-moderate penalty for explosive strength/power, larger when both are in the same session. Interference with maximal strength mainly shows up at high total volumes. For classes combining lifting and conditioning in one 60-minute session, max strength from 3 main lifts per week should be fine; power development is what gets hit most.

### Cited Findings
- **Schumann et al. 2022 (Sports Med 52(3):601-612) [Strong]:** 43 studies.
  - **Maximal strength:** concurrent vs strength-only SMD -0.06 (95% CI -0.20 to 0.09), not significant.
  - **Explosive strength:** SMD -0.28 (95% CI -0.48 to -0.08), significant. The effect appeared larger when both modes were done in the same session.
  - **Hypertrophy:** SMD -0.01, no meaningful difference.
  - **Moderators:** results held regardless of aerobic modality, frequency, training status, or age.
  - Sources: [PubMed 34757594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7)
- Schumann et al. also noted that interference with maximal strength tends to appear when overall volume is high. Lower volumes (about 2 sessions/week of each mode) showed no interference. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7)
- Follow-up analysis: concurrent training can modestly attenuate muscle *fiber* hypertrophy (possibly more with running than cycling, at least for type I fibers). — [PMC9474354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354)

### Inferences
- With 5 sessions/week, each with conditioning, total volume is the risk. Three main-lift exposures a week (one lift per day on 3 of 5 days, or spread across 5) plus accessories is within the range where max strength is not compromised.
- For power-related goals (jumping, Olympic lifts, speed work), place them before conditioning in the session and keep them modest. The same-session penalty for explosive strength is the most robust finding.
- Older adults' power declines fastest with age (and NSCA recommends power training at 40-60% 1RM). So the explosive-strength interference finding is the most relevant concurrent-training caution for a masters group class.

### Gaps
- No studies found specifically on 60-minute mixed group classes or CrossFit-style formats that measure 1RM progress with fixed vs rotating lifts.
- The order effect (strength first vs conditioning first within a session) was not quantified in what I retrieved.
