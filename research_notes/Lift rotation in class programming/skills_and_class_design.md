# Gymnastics/Skill Cycles and Group-Class Design Patterns (CrossFit / Functional Fitness)

Research date: 2026-11-22. Most named-program details sit behind paywalls or apps (CompTrain, Mayhem, Misfit, PRVN, Power Monkey, Pamela Gagnon, Gymnastics WOD), so the findings below lean on what those programs publish openly, CrossFit.com coaching articles, and a few published sample programs. Vendor marketing is marked as such.

## 1. How many skills does a cycle target, and how are skills rotated?

### Takeaway
I found no industry standard. The published models fall into three groups: (a) one skill per 4–6 week block, with a baseline test, progression and a retest (RXGymnastics, Street Parking's T2B program); (b) a multi-week gymnastics phase on the yearly calendar (HWPO's 4-week Gymnastics Phase; Invictus Masters running pulling, handstand and single-leg progressions side by side in one cycle); (c) weekly rotation of a different skill each week. None of the sources I could check explicitly described the pattern "two skills per cycle, alternating pull and push week to week."

### Cited Findings
- **One skill per 4-week cycle.** RXGymnastics' "Skills Cycle" on TrainHeroic gives every 4-week cycle to ONE skill. Complexity and intent increase week to week, the skill is tested at the end, and then the next skill starts. Sessions have Skill/Technique, Strength, Accessory and Finisher blocks, 3 days/week. — [TrainHeroic: The Skills Cycle](https://marketplace.trainheroic.com/workout-plan/team/the-skills-cycle) (from the search snippet; the page returned 404 when fetched)
- **Street Parking Toes-to-Bar program (2019, older):**
  - Format: 6 weeks × 3 sessions/week, about 24–30 min per session.
  - Session layout: mobility (2 rounds), core/grip strength, then skill work (a kip-swing buy-in and rhythm-focused T2B).
  - Testing: baseline test in Wk1 S1, retest in Wk6 S3 (active hang, hollow hold, 2-min T2B attempts).
  - Skill volume: "every 30 sec × 10 rounds" at 3→4→5→6 reps in Wks 1–4, then a 30-sec max-effort set in a 10-min EMOM (Wk5), then a 60-sec max set (Wk6).
  - Strength themes: max effort in Wks 1–3, quality under low fatigue in Wk4, quality under high fatigue in Wk5, deload in Wk6.
  - Sets end when rhythm breaks.
  - — [Street Parking T2B Program](https://streetparking.squarespace.com/write-ups/toes-to-bar-intro-redo)
- **Street Parking's main gymnastics track** posts one gymnastics session per week (Sunday), often as an EMOM of scalable drill progressions. — [Street Parking gymnastics posts](https://streetparking.squarespace.com/video/tag/Gymnastics) (2019–2021, older)
- **HWPO affiliate 2026 calendar:**
  - Gymnastics Phase: 23 Nov–20 Dec (4 weeks), "includes skill & strength focuses." It sits between a 2-week Transition and a 1-week Transition.
  - General cycles usually run 8–10 weeks. Separate Squat, Upper Body and Pulling strength phases each include a test and retest.
  - The page doesn't say how many skills the gymnastics phase covers.
  - — [HWPO Affiliate](https://www.hwpotraining.com/hwpo-affiliate)
- **Invictus Masters "Mastering the Basics" cycle** (started 28 Oct 2024, ran through the new year with holiday transition weeks):
  - Pull-ups: hollow/strict → bar muscle-ups and ring pull-ups.
  - Handstands: static holds → strict HSPU → handstand walking.
  - Single-leg: DB step-ups → pistols.
  - Snatch-position work, plus back squat, front squat, deadlift and strict press.
  - So one masters cycle runs at least three skill tracks side by side (pull, invert/press, single-leg). The post doesn't say whether they alternate by day or week.
  - — [Invictus: Mastering the Basics](https://invictusfitness.com/blog/mastering-the-basics)
- **Invictus Gymnastics (supplemental):** organized by level (1–3) with lettered blocks. Sessions are short, timed intervals (e.g., every 30 s for 6 min), and pull-up and HSPU variations appear in the same week. — [Invictus Gymnastics L2 week](https://invictusfitness.com/?p=120457); [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/equipment/invictus-gymnastics) (the review describes about 10 blocks/week across 5 days, 10–20 min each; another description says 3 × 20-min sessions/week. These conflict, probably because they describe different versions.)
- **Progressing skills across cycles rather than within one:** Mike Tromello describes gymnastics staying strict in cycle 1 (building strength), turning dynamic/kipping in cycle 2, and adding volume in cycle 3 (Open prep). He prefers 12–16-week blocks and says 8 weeks is "too short." — [Breaking Muscle, Tromello (updated 2021)](https://breakingmuscle.com/programming-for-crossfit-strength-endurance-and-preparedness/)
- **Rotating the primary modality between cycles:** Morning Chalk Up recommends rotating each cycle's primary focus. For example, a 4–8-week strength cycle is followed by a gymnastics or monostructural focus while strength is maintained, with one day per week dedicated to the cycle's focus. — [Morning Chalk Up, Joey Adduci, May 2023](https://morningchalkup.com/2023/05/08/three-reasons-to-program-training-cycles-at-your-affiliate/)
- **A different skill each week, with a dedicated gymnastics day:** this pattern was reported for one affiliate, CrossFit South Brooklyn. — [CrossFit South Brooklyn programming](https://crossfitsouthbrooklyn.com/programming) (search-snippet level only; not fetched)

### Inferences
- The best-documented pattern is "1 skill per 4–6 weeks, test → progress → retest." A gym template that wants two skills per cycle would be a small extension of documented practice. Two common-sense ways to do it, neither of them sourced as a named program's method:
  - Alternate weeks (pull skill in odd weeks, push/invert skill in even weeks). Each skill then gets about half the exposures, so a 6-week cycle gives about 3 weeks of practice per skill, below the 4–6 weeks the single-skill programs use.
  - Two days per week, one skill each. Each skill is practised every week.
- Invictus Masters putting pull, handstand and single-leg tracks in the same cycle suggests a masters audience can run 2–3 concurrent skill progressions, provided each is low-volume.
- At a 1×/week exposure (Street Parking's main track, Morning Chalk Up's "one focus day"), a skill needs a long block, or the same skill carried across multiple cycles, to show measurable progress.

### Gaps
- I couldn't access the skill-block structure of CompTrain, Mayhem (the Wodify marketplace page returned 403), Misfit, PRVN, Power Monkey/Dave Durante, Pamela Gagnon or Gymnastics WOD/The Gymnastics Bible. Their content sits behind apps or paywalls, and the search results had no description of it.
- I found no source that verifies "two skills per cycle" as a common convention, or a typical time split between two skills.
- I found no Reddit or coach-forum threads in the searches I ran.

## 2. Variety without chaos: keeping a repeating template recognizable but fresh

### Takeaway
The CrossFit HQ coaching material and affiliate-business sources lean toward a fixed class structure with cycle themes that change, communicated to members in advance. Novelty comes from rotating formats and movements inside a stable template. The retention data available is vendor-produced and doesn't isolate programming variety as a retention driver. Consistency, community and visible progress are what those sources cite instead.

### Cited Findings
- **A predictable structure is a feature.** CrossFit's "Humans Crave Structure" (Nicole Christensen, CF-L4, July 2021) recommends a standard flow:
  - The flow: whiteboard → general warm-up → specific warm-up → workout → cool-down/scores/skill.
  - It argues predictability helps newcomers and reduces skipping of disliked workouts.
  - It says a predictable flow frees about 7–10 minutes per class for skill practice.
  - Its analogy: the plan is a "coloring book" — the lines are fixed, and coaches fill them in.
  - It warns that when different coaches write different days, they must check recent days to avoid repetition.
  - — [CrossFit: Humans Crave Structure](https://crossfit.com/pro-coach/humans-crave-structure)
- **Cycles and communicated themes as the source of "freshness":** naming a cycle's focus and goals can raise member excitement, participation and retention. Short-term focuses keep athletes who often scale motivated. These are qualitative claims with no data. — [Morning Chalk Up, May 2023](https://morningchalkup.com/2023/05/08/three-reasons-to-program-training-cycles-at-your-affiliate/)
- **Novelty sits alongside benchmarks and progressions:** Two-Brain describes benchmarks, novelty and progressions as the baseline of good programming. Programming that helps retention links each client's goals to the daily workout. — [Two-Brain Radio (Vanshoyck)](https://twobrainbusiness.com/two-brain-radio-vanshoyck-challenges/)
- **Programming ranks below other retention pillars:** Chris Cooper (Two-Brain) argues retention depends more on clients staying long enough to see progress, and ranks programming below other factors. — [Two-Brain via Goodreads blog repost](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/23696519-the-five-pillars-of-retention-are-your-clients-getting-results)
- **Affiliate retention numbers (vendor data):**
  - Wodify's CFOCC 2026 presentation reportedly compares 59.2% retention for the full affiliate sample against 71.9% for the top 10%. It attributes the gap to consistency and community/connection/commitment, and quotes a top affiliate on attendance "clubs" (100/200/500 classes). — [Wodify CFOCC 2026 deck (PDF)](https://www.crossfit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/29170359/Ben-The-3-Shift-CFOCC2026.pdf). Caveat: these figures come from a search summary. The PDF is image-only and I couldn't verify the numbers or the methodology directly.
  - PushPress cites about 66% annual retention as the industry average (HFA 2025) and treats monthly churn of 3% or less as strong. — [PushPress retention guide](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/gym-member-retention-guide)
- **Member-side survey:** a Sogolytics consumer survey (Dec 2024, n=1,004 US) reports that older members value cleanliness and "variety of classes." This is general gyms, not CrossFit. — [Sogolytics study PDF](https://cdn.sogolytics.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Gym-Member-Retention-SogoStudy-Sogolytics.pdf)
- **Rotating time domains as a variety lever:** CrossFit coach material sorts workouts as very short (<5 min, heavy days), short (5–10), moderate (11–20) and long (20+). It also warns against treating every class as a "full-send" day. — [CrossFit: Programming Basics Part 1](https://crossfit.com/pro-coach/programming-basics-part-1)
- **Masters/general-population variety via format swaps:** Heidi Moody suggests turning RFT into an AMRAP with a similar cap, adjusting EMOM rep schemes, and simplifying format complexity. — [CrossFit: Coaching Aging Athletes, May 2025](https://www.crossfit.com/pro-coach/coaching-aging-crossfit-athletes-practical-guide)

### Inferences
- A practical "same structure, different stimulus" template, based on the sources above:
  - Keep the class skeleton and the weekly day-roles fixed, such as lift day, skill day and long-conditioning day.
  - Rotate conditioning formats and time domains, and the movement picked from a pool for each slot.
  - Change the cycle's named theme or skill every 4–8 weeks, and mark it with a test and retest.
- Benchmarks and retests double as a retention tool, because they make progress visible, which the Two-Brain sources say drives retention.
- Multi-coach gyms have a specific repetition risk (Christensen), so a movement-pool or rule-based generator that tracks recent use addresses a documented problem.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled or independent study isolating programming variety versus predictability as a retention driver in CrossFit gyms.
- I couldn't verify the Wodify numbers or methodology from the deck itself.
- I found no published PushPress or Wodify survey of owners on programming variety.

## 3. Masters (40+) and general-population guidance relevant to skill selection, lifts and frequency

### Takeaway
The CrossFit HQ masters guidance is qualitative:
- longer, more gradual warm-ups;
- mobility treated as "non-negotiable";
- lower-impact substitutions, with mechanics → consistency → intensity;
- about twice the recovery time between hard sessions;
- heavy lifting still encouraged.

No source I found gives a numeric gymnastics frequency for 40+. Masters programs (Invictus) still run pull-up, handstand and single-leg progressions, starting from strict and static basics.

### Cited Findings
- **CrossFit "Coaching Aging CrossFit Athletes" (Heidi Moody, CF-L3, May 21, 2025):**
  - Warm-ups should be "longer and more gradual," with extra general and specific warm-up time and transition time. Members can arrive early to warm up.
  - Mobility is "non-negotiable."
  - Swap high-impact for lower-impact options, and put form first on complex and high-impact movements.
  - On the barbell: "proper mechanics first, then consistency, before adding intensity." Many older athletes can and should lift heavy.
  - Recovery may take about twice as long as for younger athletes. Program appropriate rest days between high-intensity sessions.
  - Watch for shoulder, knee and low-back vulnerabilities, surgical hardware and cardiac medications.
  - — [CrossFit Pro Coach](https://www.crossfit.com/pro-coach/coaching-aging-crossfit-athletes-practical-guide)
- **"Aging athletes" usually means 40+,** the age at which physical changes become more noticeable. — [CrossFit "Coaching the Aging Athlete" seminar guide (PDF)](https://assets.crossfit.com/pdfs/seminars/sme_masters_trainingguide.pdf) (search snippet; not fully read)
- **Invictus Masters cycle content (Oct 2024):**
  - Strict and hollow-based pull-ups → muscle-ups.
  - Handstand holds → strict HSPU → walks.
  - Step-ups → pistols.
  - Main lifts: back squat, front squat, deadlift and strict press, with lighter, longer time-under-tension accessories and lighter snatch loads.
  - — [Invictus: Mastering the Basics](https://invictusfitness.com/blog/mastering-the-basics)
- **A gradual strict → kipping → volume progression** across cycles is also the general recommended order for gymnastics. — [Breaking Muscle, Tromello](https://breakingmuscle.com/programming-for-crossfit-strength-endurance-and-preparedness/)
- **Masters programs advertise dedicated skill work** with masters-specific progressions, e.g., PRVN Masters and WODprep Masters. These are marketing pages without frequency data. — [PRVN Masters (TrainHeroic)](https://marketplace.trainheroic.com/workout-plan/team/prvn-masters); [WODprep Masters](https://wodprep.com/?p=6868)

### Inferences
- For a 40+ group, the safest skill choices are strict and static progressions: hollow, strict pulling, handstand holds/wall work, step-up → pistol. Kipping or inversion-heavy volume can come later in the block or in later cycles.
- Longer warm-ups for masters eat into a 60-minute budget, which makes it harder to fit a full lift plus a full skill block on the same day.
- "Twice the recovery" suggests not stacking high-intensity days back to back. The skill day can double as a lower-intensity day.

### Gaps
- I found no numeric guidance on gymnastics sessions per week, or lift frequency, specifically for 40+ class members.
- I didn't read the CrossFit masters seminar guide PDF in full.
- I found nothing usable from "Aging Athlete" or "Rx Masters" sources.

## 4. 60-minute class time budgets: can one class hold both a lift and a skill?

### Takeaway
CrossFit HQ coaching guidance (Aug 2024) argues against routinely stacking heavy strength and a met-con in one class. It says skill practice should not be squeezed into the warm-up to make room for heavy lifting. A consistent class flow frees about 7–10 minutes for skill. Published example budgets put skill at about 10 minutes and a strength piece under about 20 minutes. Fitting a full lift, a skill and a met-con in 60 minutes is tight, and masters warm-ups make it tighter.

### Cited Findings
- **Pete Shaw, "Ask a Coach" (CrossFit, Aug 7, 2024):**
  - Pairing strength and a met-con every class limits maximal lifting and maximal met-con performance, reduces skill practice and cuts coaching time.
  - Skill work (pull-ups, HSPU) shouldn't be squeezed into a short warm-up to make room for heavy lifts.
  - Spare time should go to a thoughtful warm-up and a coach-led teaching segment with progressions and repetition.
  - — [CrossFit: Strength and Met-Con](https://crossfit.com/pro-coach/strength-and-metcon)
- **About 7–10 minutes per class** freed for skill practice by a predictable class flow. — [CrossFit: Humans Crave Structure (2021)](https://crossfit.com/pro-coach/humans-crave-structure)
- **Example session budget (2009, old):** warm-up 10, skill 10, cool-down 10, strength under 20, met-con under 20, about 80 minutes total. Skill time targets weaknesses. — [ybrikman CrossFit strength/power hybrid (2009)](https://ybrikman.com/blog/2009/07/12/crossfit-strengthpower-hybrid)
- **An EMOM that alternates skill and accessory work** (odd minutes: 30–40 s of gymnastics skill; even minutes: pull-aparts and hollow rocks) is one way to embed skill work in a strength day efficiently. — [EliteFTS template](https://elitefts.com/education/why-strength-training-is-equally-important-as-conditioning-with-template)
- **Commercial 60-minute class programming:**
  - CompTrain Gym provides time-stamped lesson plans for 60-minute classes (timeline, workout brief starting at 0:00–5:00, general and specific warm-up, daily focus) — [CompTrain Gym](https://programs.btwb.com/comptrain/gym).
  - HWPO CLASS is 60 minutes with warm-ups plus a two-part workout. Accessory or extra work sits outside the 60 minutes — [HWPO Affiliate](https://www.hwpotraining.com/hwpo-affiliate).
- **Stand-alone skill sessions in published programs run about 20–30 minutes:** Street Parking T2B at 24–30 min ([source](https://streetparking.squarespace.com/write-ups/toes-to-bar-intro-redo)), and Invictus Gymnastics blocks at about 10–20 min ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/equipment/invictus-gymnastics)).

### Inferences
- A rough 60-minute budget consistent with these sources:
  - brief/whiteboard about 5;
  - warm-up 10–15 (towards 15 for masters);
  - one "A" piece of about 15–20 (either a lift or a skill);
  - met-con 10–20;
  - cool-down about 5.
- Fitting both a full lift and a full skill block on the same day means either a short met-con or a "B" skill piece of about 8–10 min (e.g., an EMOM skill/accessory alternation), not two full blocks.
- One way to satisfy the HQ advice is to alternate lift days and skill days within the weekly template, rather than stacking them. A low-volume skill EMOM can still go on lift days if time allows.

### Gaps
- I couldn't see the minute-by-minute CompTrain or HWPO timelines (paywalled).
- I found no survey of typical affiliate class-block durations.
