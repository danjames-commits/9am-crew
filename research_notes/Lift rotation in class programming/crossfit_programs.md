# Lift rotation in CrossFit affiliate/class programming services (cycle structure of strength lifts and skills)

Research date: 2026-11-22. About 25 tool calls. Many program pages are paywalled, returned 403/404, or only exist as image/compressed PDFs, so coverage is uneven. Training Think Tank (TTT) gave the most detail. Several programs (Misfit, Invictus, Linchpin, crossfit.com/CrossFit Affiliate Programming, Pioneer) yielded nothing citable in this pass and appear only under Gaps.

## Q1. Per-program structure: cycle length, number of primary lifts, fixed vs rotating days, A/B weeks, frequency, Olympic lifts, gymnastics skills

### Takeaway
None of the documented programs runs the same 3 lifts on fixed weekdays all year. The common pattern is a cycle of about 6–12 weeks in which a small set of barbell lifts (usually 2–4 slow lifts plus one Olympic-lift slot) each get one fixed slot per week. The lift set changes from cycle to cycle, and sometimes partway through a cycle. TTT goes further, alternating different lifts and skills on the same weekday in A/B weeks. Within a cycle, fixed weekly slots are the norm. Across cycles, the pool rotates.

### Cited Findings

Each cycle has its own strength, Olympic-lifting and gymnastics emphasis. Examples: Apex Open Prep is "Clean complex, CrossFit Total, upper body strength-endurance" with gymnastics "Pull-ups, TTB, wall walks". Novus Phase 2 is "Linear progressions on slow lifts" plus "Olympic lift reintroduction". Persist is "High-intensity barbell work" with a snatch technical focus and "Heavier weighted gymnastics, GHD sit-ups, rope climbs". Ultimus is a "Fatigue-resistant squat max" plus push press/jerk speed and "Static handstands/handstand walking". — [TTT Affiliate 2026 Annual Outlook](https://trainingthinktank.com/ttt-affiliate-2026-annual-outlook/)
- Resurgence cycle (8 weeks, Jul 21–Sep 8, 2025) explicitly alternates A and B weeks. Mon: Clean & Jerk linear progression "building toward a 1-rep max" (both weeks). Tue: A = Toes-to-Bar strength/grip, B = Weighted Pull-Ups. Wed: A = skill/coaching focus + metcon, B = bench press strength. Thu: A = Weighted Pull-Ups, B = Core/Compression. Counting from the same source.

### Inferences
- The dominant documented pattern is "fixed slot within a cycle, rotating pool across cycles." Each cycle has about 2–3 slow lifts, each trained once a week on a roughly fixed day, plus one Olympic-lift slot that often alternates snatch/C&J weekly. Over a year the pool covers about 6–8+ lifts. Mayhem Surge, JH CrossFit and CompTrain Base Camp blocks all fit this.
- TTT differs: A/B alternating weeks put a different lift or skill on the same weekday. Slow lifts then get about one exposure per 2 weeks, while Olympic lifts get weekly exposure. Olympic lifts are a main class strength slot in TTT and Mayhem-style cycles, not an afterthought.
- CompTrain's Base Camp shows a mid-cycle swap of variants (back squat to front squat, bench to strict press, conventional to sumo deadlift). That rotates the lift pool every about 4 weeks while keeping the same movement-pattern slots (squat / press / hinge).
- "Same 3 lifts on fixed weekdays all year" was not observed in any service. The 3-lift Mon/Wed/Fri shape appears only within single cycles (Mayhem Surge: squat / deadlift / Oly).

## Q3. Stated reasons for fixed vs rotating structures

### Takeaway
Reasons given for fixed, repeated cycles: measurable progress and retesting, technique mastery, and member motivation and retention. Reasons given for rotation: avoiding plateaus, boredom and overuse injury; coping with variable attendance; and the "general physical preparedness" (GPP) mandate. Programmers mostly split the difference: fixed lifts within a short cycle, rotating between cycles, often with retests at the end.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Chalk Up on cycles. Retention: members who see tangible improvement stay "motivated and invested in the training". Movement mastery: "Safety, efficacy and efficiency are directly tied to the quality of movement". Measurable results, citing the CrossFit L2 guide's warning about "a lack of regular assessment". It suggests "four to eight weeks getting strong", with one day a week dedicated to the focus ("training") and varied days ("exercise") around it. — [Morning Chalk Up, "Three Reasons to Program Training Cycles at Your Affiliate" (2023)](https://morningchalkup.com/2023/05/08/three-reasons-to-program-training-cycles-at-your-affiliate/)
- Box Programming (Jason) against linear percentage cycles in groups: attendance varies, "'Peaking' is NOT realistic", "Using percentages is a great way to remove the fun from your classes and confuse your clients", and repetition breeds plateaus, boredom and overuse. Instead: "we rotate variations for max effort and dynamic effort regularly", changing movements about every 3 weeks. — [Box Programming, "Why Cycles Suck For Group Training"](https://boxprogramming.com/cycles-suck) (undated)
- Box Programming says "strength" days are seldom used in traditional box models; in one tracked month there were only four "strength-only" days. — [Box Programming, Strength Training for CrossFit Classes](https://boxprogramming.com/importanceofstrength) (via search summary)
- TTT cycle rationales are periodization and calendar driven: Open prep, then post-Open rebuild ("Murph prep"), then "Targets individual bottlenecks", off-season strength, then in-season density. One TTT path "will be more focused on GPP than linear progressions" with fewer test/retests. — [TTT 2026 Outlook](https://trainingthinktank.com/ttt-affiliate-2026-annual-outlook/); [TTT programs (via search snippet)](https://programs.trainingthinktank.com/ttt-affiliate)
- Mayhem Surge: wave loading "without overreaching"; short sessions to fit class schedules. — [Mayhem Surge doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1O1LePVZLBi5_8f9oK2drdvTd1v0YUkTbh_MZbOpCImI/mobilebasic)
- JH CrossFit: the 20-rep squat builds strength and "mental toughness". The bench burnout targets upper-body endurance. Olympic work is for technique and cycling: "It's not a max-out cycle, but you'll see consistent progression over the 10 weeks." — [JH CrossFit](https://www.jhcrossfit.com/blog/2025/3/24/strength-cycle)
- A third-party affiliate blog on outsourced programming says it "makes sure you don't squat heavy three days in a row". — [CrossFit Aggieland blog (Dec 2025, upd. Sep 2026)](https://www.crossfitaggieland.com/blog/mayhem-programming-explained)

### Inferences
- Coach simplicity and member familiarity were not explicitly cited by any service found. Recurring weekly slots plus clear test/retest weeks (TTT, Mayhem Surge, CompTrain) suggest that progression tracking is the main driver for keeping slots fixed within a cycle.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes from Ben Bergeron/CompTrain, Mayhem staff, Misfit or Invictus programmers on fixed vs rotating were found.

## Q4. How common is each pattern?

### Takeaway
I found no count, survey or estimate of how many affiliate programs use fixed-day 3-lift structures versus rotating pools or A/B weeks.

### Cited Findings
- A coaching site notes that class members are "at the mercy of whatever programming" the gym follows, and that many gyms follow strength cycles that may not target the lifts a member wants. — [PowerliftingTechnique, CrossFit powerlifting program](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/crossfit-powerlifting-program/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- From this small sample (TTT, CompTrain, Mayhem-adjacent, JH CrossFit, PRVN packet, Street Parking POWER), the most common shape is 2–4 main lifts per cycle, each trained once a week, with the pool rotating every 4–10 weeks. Explicit A/B week alternation is documented only at TTT. This is a qualitative read, not a statistic.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/crossfit, r/crossfitaffiliates) were surfaced by search. No industry survey on structure prevalence was found.
